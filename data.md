
# Data & Visualisation Notes

## Project

**Decision Analysis: Wage Theft Among International Students in Australia**

This document records the data sources, data status, visual representations and limitations associated with this case study. It distinguishes illustrative data from historical contextual information and documents the limitations of the visual analysis.

---

## 1. Reported Wage-Theft Cases Trend (2018–2023)

- **Visualisation type:** Line chart
- **Data status:** Illustrative
- **File:** `visuals/reported-cases-trend.png`

This visualisation demonstrates how a time-series chart may be used to represent a hypothetical trend in reported wage-theft cases over time.

The figures used to generate the chart are illustrative and have not been independently verified against an official Australian dataset.

**Data limitation:** The values must not be interpreted as actual reported wage-theft cases, official statistics or evidence of an increasing trend in wage theft.

## 3. Australian National Minimum Wage Trend (2018–2022)

- **Visualisation type:** Line chart
- **Data status:** Historical contextual data
- **File:** `visuals/national-minimum-wage-trend.png`

This visualisation presents the historical Australian national minimum wage hourly rate over the selected period.

The chart provides wage-policy context relevant to the discussion of employment conditions.

Historical figures and supporting references are documented in `references.md`.

**Data limitation:** The national minimum wage is not necessarily the applicable rate for every worker. Award rates, classifications and other employment conditions may determine applicable pay. The visualisation does not represent actual wages received by international students or measure wage theft.

---

## 4. Data Integrity and Limitations

- Illustrative data are clearly identified as illustrative.
- Illustrative values are not presented as empirical findings.
- Historical contextual data are distinguished from wage-theft statistics.
- Conceptual analysis is not presented as statistical evidence.
- Visualisations should not imply statistical certainty where underlying datasets are unavailable.
- Supporting references and sources should be documented in `references.md`.
- The visualisations do not establish causal relationships.

---

## 5. Future Improvements

The project could be strengthened through the incorporation of verified datasets from reputable sources, including Australian government agencies, academic research and relevant worker advocacy organisations.

Potential improvements include:

- Verifying reported wage-theft case data against authoritative sources.
- Obtaining verified employment-sector data.
- Incorporating reliable data on workplace compliance and underpayment.
- Developing evidence-supported comparisons of intervention effectiveness.
- Documenting dataset collection methods, definitions and time periods.
- Conducting further quantitative analysis where reliable data are available.

## Overall Data Limitations

The first two visualisations use illustrative data rather than verified empirical datasets. The third visualisation presents historical minimum wage information as contextual data.

These visualisations serve different analytical purposes and should not be interpreted as directly comparable evidence.

The illustrative charts demonstrate data presentation and analytical communication techniques rather than establishing factual conclusions about the prevalence, distribution or progression of wage theft in Australia.

Further research using verified datasets would be required to support empirical conclusions.
