# Data & Visualisation Notes

## Project
**Decision Analysis: Wage Theft Among International Students in Australia**

This document records the datasets and visual representations associated with the case study. It provides transparency regarding data interpretation, limitations and the distinction between verified evidence and illustrative figures.

## 1. Reported Wage-Theft Cases (2018–2023)

The original academic visualisation presented an increasing trend in reported wage-theft cases between 2018 and 2023.

The chart was intended to illustrate the scale and progression of the problem over time.

**Data verification status:** The original figures have not yet been independently verified against their underlying source. They should therefore not be interpreted as official statistics.

## 2. Employment Sectors

The original visualisation categorised potential wage-theft exposure across employment sectors:

- Hospitality
- Retail
- Agriculture
- Other sectors

The visualisation was intended to illustrate the distribution of employment sectors associated with the case study.

**Data verification status:** Sector percentages require verification against their original dataset or source before being presented as empirical findings.

## 3. Wage Comparison

The original case study included a comparison between student wages and legal minimum wages over time.

This visualisation was intended to demonstrate the potential difference between wages received and legally applicable minimum wage rates.

**Data verification status:** The original numerical values and assumptions require verification, including the applicable wage rates, dates, employment conditions and source.

## 4. Decision-Making Process

A conceptual visualisation was included to illustrate the relationship between employer decisions, cognitive biases and wage underpayment.

This is a conceptual model rather than a statistical dataset.

## 5. Intervention Effectiveness

The original visualisation compared potential intervention approaches, including:

- Awareness campaigns
- Legal support
- Government audits

The chart was intended to compare possible mitigation approaches.

**Data verification status:** The percentages presented in the original visualisation have not been independently verified. They must not be interpreted as measured intervention effectiveness without supporting research.

## 6. Data Integrity & Limitations

- Original figures require source verification.
- Illustrative figures must be clearly labelled.
- Conceptual diagrams are not empirical evidence.
- Charts should not imply statistical certainty where underlying datasets are unavailable.
- Supporting references should be documented in `references.md`.

## 7. Future Improvements

The case study may be strengthened through the incorporation of verified data from reputable sources, including Australian government agencies, academic research and relevant worker advocacy organisations.

## Visualisation Data Summary

### Visualisation 1 — Reported Wage-Theft Cases Trend
- **Type:** Line chart
- **Data status:** Illustrative, not verified against an official dataset.
- **Purpose:** Demonstrates how a time-series trend could be used to examine reported wage-theft cases.
- **Limitation:** Values must not be interpreted as actual reported case counts.

### Visualisation 2 — Employment Sector Distribution
- **Type:** Pie chart
- **Data status:** Illustrative, not verified against an official dataset.
- **Purpose:** Demonstrates how employment sectors could be represented in a distribution chart.
- **Limitation:** Percentages must not be interpreted as actual employment-sector statistics.

### Visualisation 3 — Australian National Minimum Wage Trend (2018–2022)
- **Type:** Line chart
- **Data status:** Historical national minimum wage figures used as a baseline.
- **Source:** Fair Work Commission historical national minimum wage orders and Annual Wage Review material, documented in `references.md`.
- **Purpose:** Provides historical wage-policy context relevant to the discussion of employment conditions.
- **Limitation:** The national minimum wage is not necessarily the applicable rate for every worker. Award rates, classifications and employment conditions may differ. The chart does not represent international students' actual wages.

### Overall Data Limitations
- Visualisations 1 and 2 use illustrative data rather than verified empirical datasets.
- Visualisation 3 represents historical minimum wage baseline figures, not wage-theft prevalence or student wage outcomes.
- The visualisations serve different analytical purposes and should not be directly interpreted as evidence of a causal relationship.
- Further research should use verified datasets from authoritative sources to establish empirical findings.
- Any conclusions drawn from illustrative visualisations should be treated as conceptual rather than statistically validated.
