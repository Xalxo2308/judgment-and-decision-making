
# References & Data Sources

This document records the academic context, references, data sources and supporting materials associated with the case study **Decision Analysis: Wage Theft Among International Students in Australia**.

The reference register distinguishes documented sources from data that remain unverified. References and datasets are being consolidated as part of the reconstruction and refinement of the original academic assignment for portfolio publication.

---

## 1. Academic Context

- **Subject:** 49001 – Judgment & Decision Making
- **Institution:** University of Technology Sydney (UTS)
- **Project:** Decision Analysis: Wage Theft Among International Students in Australia

The project applies decision-analysis concepts to the examination of wage theft affecting international students in Australia, including stakeholder analysis, intervention alternatives, qualitative evaluation and methodological limitations.

## 5. Decision-Making Frameworks and Analytical Methodology

The project applies a structured decision-analysis approach involving:

- Problem identification.
- Stakeholder analysis.
- Consideration of behavioural and cognitive factors.
- Identification of decision alternatives.
- Development of evaluation criteria.
- Qualitative assessment of alternatives.
- Recognition of uncertainty and methodological limitations.

The qualitative decision matrix in this project represents a preliminary analytical assessment. It does not constitute an empirically validated decision model.

Further supporting academic references relating to specific behavioural concepts, cognitive biases or decision-analysis frameworks may be added as the original assignment sources are reviewed and verified.

## 6. Policy and Intervention Context

The project considers potential intervention approaches involving:

- Worker education and awareness.
- Reporting and support mechanisms.
- Targeted enforcement.
- Data collection and information sharing.
- Integrated intervention strategies.

The alternatives are examined conceptually through the decision-analysis framework.

The project does not claim that the qualitative assessments establish the actual effectiveness of these interventions.

Additional policy, regulatory and empirical sources may be incorporated as the analysis is further developed.

## 7. Data Integrity and Limitations

- Statistical data must be supported by identifiable sources before being presented as verified empirical findings.
- Illustrative figures are explicitly labelled.
- Conceptual diagrams are not statistical evidence.
- Historical minimum wage information is distinguished from wage-theft prevalence.
- Qualitative ratings represent analytical judgements rather than measured outcomes.
- References should be traceable to their original sources.
- Unverified original assignment figures are not presented as independently confirmed data.
